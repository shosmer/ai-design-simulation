The data

Eleven sources, four selection rules, and the rejects. If you trust nothing else here, audit this page.

Selection rules, applied to everything here: a source must be primary (from the original collector), machine-readable (the pipeline refreshes it without a human), recurring (a time series, not a snapshot PDF), and free to verify (anyone can re-pull the same numbers). Scope: U.S. tech labor market, except the AI-usage and web-quality sources, which are global.

Labor market

📋Indeed job postings indices (aggregate + by sector) and AI-mention share
weekly (postings), monthly (AI tracker) · last pulled 2026-06-11 · 200,464 rows

Why this source: The only postings index that's free, machine-readable (plain CSVs on GitHub, CC-BY), and updated weekly. Sector-level granularity lets us build the design-vs-aggregate ratio that nets out the macro cycle — the difference-in-differences trick the whole backcast rests on.

Watch out: Indeed's taxonomy has no 'design' sector; we proxy with four design-adjacent sectors. Postings measure hiring intent, not hires.

Feeds: Backcast fit, design + management postings signals, check-in brief.

🏛️BLS OEWS national employment and wage estimates for design occupations
annual (spring release of prior-May estimates) · last pulled 2026-06-11 · 8 rows

Why this source: The ground truth. BLS occupation-level employment counts and full wage distributions are the only numbers here with survey-grade methodology — every simulated headcount and wage is anchored to them.

Watch out: Annual, ~12-month lag. SOC codes lag reality: 'web and digital interface designers' is the closest thing to product designers.

Feeds: Model scale and wage calibration; designer and IT-manager baselines.

📉Tech layoff events (company, headcount, date) via layoffs.fyi
continuous · last pulled 2026-06-11 · 4,448 rows

Why this source: The de-facto public record of tech layoffs, event-level since 2020. We scrape its Airtable shared view directly so the series updates without manual exports — and it powers the story's most disarming fact: the all-time peak predates design-capable AI.

Watch out: ~1/3 of events lack headcounts (totals undercount); attribution from counts alone is impossible; the scrape is fragile by nature.

Feeds: Layoffs story scene, signals card, check-in brief.

⚖️PM vs designer openings from public Greenhouse/Lever/Ashby boards
continuous (one snapshot per pull; panel of ~40 tech companies) · last pulled 2026-06-12 · 34 rows

Why this source: Built after the tracker behind the public PM-vs-design debate proved bot-walled and unverifiable. Greenhouse/Lever/Ashby publish company job boards as open JSON — primary sources. A fixed ~40-company panel gives us the PM:designer ratio with a documented title classifier.

Watch out: Panel skews infra-heavy, so the level runs above market-wide estimates; the direction across snapshots is the signal. Series starts June 2026.

Feeds: Role-mix signals card, check-in brief.

Adoption & usage

🏢Census BTOS biweekly AI use by sector and firm size
biweekly · last pulled 2026-06-11 · 301 rows

Why this source: The only nationally representative, high-frequency (biweekly) measure of firm AI adoption — 1.2M businesses surveyed by the Census Bureau. It calibrates the model's entire adoption curve.

Watch out: Question wording changed since 2023 (production use vs any business function); national figures need a ~2x tech-sector scaling.

Feeds: Firm adoption-start distribution (backcast), signals card.

🔭Anthropic Economic Index: task-level AI usage, automation vs augmentation
roughly quarterly · last pulled 2026-06-11 · 672,873 rows

Why this source: The only public dataset mapping real AI usage to official O*NET task statements — which is what lets capability curves be anchored to measured behavior per task category instead of guessed. Also our individual-usage concentration measure (178 countries).

Watch out: Observes one assistant (Claude), which is text-first — visual-production automation is undercounted; usage is not the same as work performed.

Feeds: Capability-curve anchoring, categories page, adoption-concentration card.

💸Per-token AI prices (OpenRouter catalog) + ECI wage index
continuous (price series grows one snapshot per pull); ECI quarterly · last pulled 2026-06-11 · 438 rows

Why this source: No historical API for AI prices exists, so we built our own: each pull snapshots ~340 models' per-token prices from OpenRouter's open catalog. It is the tripwire for the model's cost-decline assumption — and for the capex-subsidy reversal scenario.

Watch out: Accumulates from June 2026; also carries the wage comparison series (Employment Cost Index via FRED).

Feeds: AI-cost-decline assumption, price story scene, signals card.

Demand & economy

🍽️Design purchased (SAS revenue via FRED) and shipped (Google Play releases)
annual (revenue), monthly (app releases) · last pulled 2026-06-12 · 1,787 rows

Why this source: The elasticity question itself, measured three ways: Census revenue for design industries (purchased design), Google Play releases (designed output shipped), and Census business formation (the extensive margin — new companies as future design consumers). Plus the fed funds rate, because appetite tracks capital conditions (corr −0.5 since 2020).

Watch out: No PPI exists for design services (revenue can't split price from quantity); app releases absorb platform-policy shocks; formation filings are not employer firms.

Feeds: Evidence ratio, formation line, regime watch — the story's closing scenes.

📐Lighthouse accessibility score percentiles (HTTP Archive)
monthly · last pulled 2026-06-12 · 176 rows

Why this source: The good-enough-vs-arms-race discriminator needs the *distribution* of design quality over time, and HTTP Archive's Lighthouse percentiles across millions of real sites are the only public series shaped like that — floor (p10) vs ceiling (p90), monthly since 2017.

Watch out: Technical quality only (no Lighthouse for judgment); the ceiling is censored near the scale max, so an arms race wouldn't show here.

Feeds: Quality story scene, gap signals card, check-in good-enough watch.

Mapping

🗺️O*NET task statements (task text -> SOC occupation mapping)
quarterly database releases; content changes slowly · last pulled 2026-06-11 · 18,796 rows

Why this source: The official task statements per occupation — the join key that turns AEI's task-level usage into design-occupation capability anchors without keyword guessing (which, when we tried it, matched garment and database design).

Watch out: Task statements update slowly and lag how design work actually changes.

Feeds: Category maturity profile behind the capability curves.

Stubs

💰Product designer compensation by company/level (official API, access-gated)
continuous · last pulled 2026-06-11

Why this source: Designer compensation by company and level would calibrate wage dynamics and provide the premium-segment price series the quality discriminator is missing. Official API access is request-gated; the adapter waits.

Watch out: Not yet active.

Feeds: (future) wage-by-seniority calibration, premium-price watch.

Sources we rejected, and why

  • TrueUp — the tracker behind the public PM-vs-design debate — Cloudflare-walled against both plain and headless fetching, so its numbers can't be independently verified or refreshed. We rebuilt the ratio from the primary job boards instead.
  • Statista and stat-aggregator blogs — paywalled or unsourced re-aggregations; every number we use must trace to a primary collector.
  • LinkedIn Economic Graph — rich but access-gated and methodologically opaque; no machine-readable public series.
  • AIGA Design Census — discontinued after 2019 — predates everything this model is about.
  • One-off vendor surveys — (state-of-X PDFs) cited as context in the story where verified, but never wired into the pipeline: no consistent time series, shifting methodology, marketing incentives.

Annual reports we read but don't wire in (no machine-readable series): Stanford HAI AI Index (cited for the 280x inference-price decline), UX Tools Design Tools Survey, NN/g State of UX. Everything wired in refreshes with one command; this page regenerates from the live source registry and pull manifest, so it cannot drift from what the pipeline actually does.
